# Model tiers

A **tier** rates the **model (series)**, not one chair: how high it sits in its maker's range. The condition and age of a chair, its price in the Philippines and its used price never change the tier. The website's **value** rating is a separate question: whether the price asked is fair.

## Why this scale

- **There is no official industry tier.** The Japanese standards for office chairs (JIS S 1032 and JIS S 1043, written by the Japan Office Furniture Association, JOIFA) and the US standard (BIFMA X5.1) are pass/fail tests for size, strength and safety. The JOIFA "standard use period" (8 years for swivel chairs) is a service-life guide, the same for every model.
- **Makers' catalogues sort chairs by use** (task, meeting, executive), not by grade. The only grading words are in makers' own launch press releases, e.g. Okamura calls chairs "ミドルクラス" (middle class) or "スタンダード" (standard), and in the list price (メーカー希望小売価格) the maker sets.
- **Japanese offices do rank chairs by job title, informally and by features**: staff get chairs without arms, section and department heads get armrests and a higher back, executives get leather. It is a custom, not a written standard, and it is about the features of one chair, not about which model is better.
- So this scale uses only those two official things: **the maker's own class word**, and otherwise **the maker's list price for a new chair**. The price bands are set from Okamura's own labelled chairs (Contessa II "flagship", Feego and Escudo "middle class", Carrozza "standard").

## How a tier is decided

1. **Maker's own class word** (press release, catalogue, product page), quoted with the link. If it exists, it wins.
2. Otherwise **list price new**, in yen including tax, for the base setup (no headrest, cheapest arm option). Where no maker list price was found, a Japan shop price for a new chair is used and the row says so.
3. Price bands: Flagship ¥150,000 and up; Middle ¥80,000 to ¥149,999; Standard ¥40,000 to ¥79,999; Economy under ¥40,000.

**Reference** rows are models not in the library and not seen at Office Busters Philippines. They are shown only to mark where the top of a maker's range is.

## Tier scale

| Tier | Name | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flagship | The maker's top chairs: its own "flagship", or list price ¥150,000 and up. |
| 2 | Middle | The maker's "middle class" (ミドルクラス), or list price ¥80,000 to ¥149,999. |
| 3 | Standard | The baseline: the normal office task chair a big maker sells for staff, its own "standard" (スタンダード), or list price ¥40,000 to ¥79,999. |
| 4 | Economy | Below the baseline: budget, home (SOHO) and compact chairs, list price under ¥40,000. |

## Notes

- **Feego mesh vs Escudo mesh (checked 26 Nov 2026, Okamura press releases):** Okamura called both "middle class" at launch (Feego mesh, Nov 2007: "汎用性の高いミドルクラスのメッシュシーティング"; Escudo, Nov 2009: "ミドルクラスのオフィスシーティング"), so they are the **same tier**. Starting prices before tax: Feego mesh ¥74,600 (2008), Escudo mesh ¥61,000 (2011), so the Feego mesh cost about 22% more. The Escudo mesh has more adjustment: seat slide 50 mm (Feego mesh 45 mm in 4 steps) and an add-on lumbar support that moves 60 mm. Recline (ankle-tilt) and height range (92 mm) are the same. Okamura's product page today calls the Escudo "ワンランク上のスタンダードモデル" (a standard model, one rank up).
- **Not placed yet:** Steelcase Series 1 and Series 2 (Steelcase calls them affordable, "手の届く価格", but no Japan price was found); Steelcase Think and Karman; Itoki Torino (no new price, sold from 1999); chairs whose model is not identified yet.
